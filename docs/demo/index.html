NASDAQ TotalView-ITCH 5.0 · trading day of 30 January 2019

Tachyon

A C++20 trading core in two halves: a feed handler that rebuilds every symbol's limit order book from a full day of NASDAQ's binary feed, and a price-time matching engine that decides trades itself. Lock-free threads, zero allocation on the hot path, and every claim on this page traced to a result file.

RTwo jobs that look alike

Rebuilding a book is not matching one

Feed handler · book builder

Rebuild what the exchange published

ITCH is NASDAQ's output. Every execution in it already happened at the exchange, so replaying it only removes the shares the feed says were filled. Nothing is matched.

  • Zero-copy decode of 10 message types from a memory-mapped file
  • One book per stock locate, 65,536 slots
  • U replace: delete the old id, add the new one, keep side and symbol
Matching engine

Decide the trades

The engine accepts orders and produces trades with price-time priority. It reuses the book builder's pooled orders, level FIFOs and id map.

  • Limit, market and IOC orders; cancel; modify
  • Shrinking an order keeps its place; any other change loses it
  • Ack, Trade, Cancelled and Rejected events, in order
AThe full day

368 million messages, every book empty at the close

The replay rebuilt books for symbols with no reference to an unknown order. The message mix is dominated by adds and deletes, the churn the order pool and id map are built for.

Every crossed book had a reason

A correct rebuild of NASDAQ's own book should never cross during continuous trading. Across the day the book crossed or locked in 8 episodes, all while the symbol was paused (limit-up/limit-down) or quoting only, according to its ITCH trading-action messages.

The rings use release stores and acquire loads on head and tail, each on its own 64-byte line, with each side caching the other's index. The threaded pipeline produced the same books as a single-threaded run at mid-day checkpoints and the same best-price changes in the same order. Both ThreadSanitizer jobs in CI pass.

XDesign choices, measured

Why each structure is the one it is

Order id map vs std::unordered_map

Open addressing keeps a lookup in one or two adjacent slots, and backward-shift deletion means a day of deletes leaves no tombstones. Both maps were reserved for one million ids before timing.

Price levels and ring padding

Ring padding only paid off with the consumer on the CPU's other 8-core die. Two padded cross-die runs took about 1.3 s, probably a parked core waking; they are kept in the data.

DLimits

What this does not show

  • One trading day and one machine: a laptop with no isolated cores, turbo on and core parking enabled, so tail latency is worse than on a tuned server.
  • Book updates miss the 100 ns median target. A full day keeps up to orders live, which does not fit in cache. Hardware counters were not available on Windows, so that explanation is an inference.
  • Timestamps come from rdtsc, which advances in roughly 10 ns steps on this CPU.
  • The engine handles one symbol, with no self-trade prevention, auctions or risk checks.
  • The backtest demo ( fills, marked to mid) ignores queue position. It demonstrates the bindings and says nothing about any strategy.
